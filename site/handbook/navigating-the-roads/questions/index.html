Section 6

Navigating the Roads: practice questions

Traffic Lanes

  • What does a single solid yellow line on a road mark?

    • Lanes going the same direction on a one-way road.
    • The center of a road with two-way traffic.
    • The edge of a lane that is about to end.
    Answer

    The center of a road with two-way traffic.

    A single solid yellow line marks the center of a two-way road. Lanes going the same direction are separated by white lines.

  • On a two-lane road, a solid yellow line runs on your side of the center. What does it tell you about passing?

    • You may not pass the car ahead.
    • You may pass if no one is coming.
    • You may pass a car below the limit.
    Answer

    You may not pass the car ahead.

  • When may you turn left across a single set of double yellow lines?

    • Never; they form a barrier you can't cross.
    • Only where there is a marked opening.
    • To enter a driveway or make a U-turn.
    Answer

    To enter a driveway or make a U-turn.

    You may turn left across one set of double yellow lines into or out of a driveway or private road, or to make a U-turn. Only two sets two feet apart form a barrier.

  • Construction signs tell you to use the other side of the road because your side is closed. What should you do?

    • Follow the signs, driving left of the double yellow lines.
    • Stay right of the double yellow lines and wait it out.
    • Turn around, since crossing double yellow is never allowed.
    Answer

    Follow the signs, driving left of the double yellow lines.

  • What is indicated by two sets of double yellow lines spaced 2 feet or more apart?

    • Lanes of traffic moving in the same direction.
    • Barrier.
    • Carpool/High-Occupancy (HOV) lane.
    Answer

    Barrier.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #3

  • Two sets of double yellow lines, two or more feet apart, separate you from a driveway on the left. May you turn left into it?

    • Yes, since it is a private driveway.
    • Only at a marked opening in the lines.
    • Yes, after yielding to oncoming traffic.
    Answer

    Only at a marked opening in the lines.

  • A broken yellow line runs next to your lane, with no solid line on your side. What does it mean?

    • You may never pass on this stretch.
    • Only oncoming traffic may pass here.
    • You may pass, but only when it is safe.
    Answer

    You may pass, but only when it is safe.

  • Double solid white lines separate your lane from a carpool lane. When may you move into it?

    • Not until the lines change to a single broken line.
    • Whenever you have enough people in the car.
    • Whenever traffic is clear and you have signaled.
    Answer

    Not until the lines change to a single broken line.

  • Broken white lines separate the lanes on your road. What do they tell you?

    • They mark the center of a two-way road.
    • Traffic in both lanes goes the same way.
    • You may not change lanes across them.
    Answer

    Traffic in both lanes goes the same way.

    Broken white lines separate lanes going in the same direction, and you may change lanes across them when safe. Yellow lines separate opposite directions.

  • Your freeway lane is marked with large broken lines. What should you be ready for?

    • The lane may exit the freeway or end.
    • A carpool lane is about to begin on the left.
    • Passing is now allowed on both sides.
    Answer

    The lane may exit the freeway or end.

  • What do white triangles like these across your lane show?

    • That a speed bump is ahead.
    • Where to yield or stop.
    • Where a bike lane crosses.
    Answer

    Where to yield or stop.

Choosing a Lane

  • On a freeway, which lane is the Number 1 Lane?

    • The far-right lane.
    • The far-left, or fast, lane.
    • The lane nearest the on-ramp.
    Answer

    The far-left, or fast, lane.

  • You are pulling away from the curb onto a street with several lanes. Which lane should you enter?

    • The left lane.
    • The center lane.
    • The right lane.
    Answer

    The right lane.

    Use the right lane to enter or exit traffic or to pull out from a curb or shoulder. The left lane is for passing and left turns.

Changing Lanes

  • Before changing lanes, you have signaled and checked your mirrors. What else should you do?

    • Slow down to let the other lane pass you.
    • Look over your shoulder toward the new lane.
    • Change lanes; mirrors cover the lane beside you.
    Answer

    Look over your shoulder toward the new lane.

    Look over your shoulder in the direction you are moving to check your blind spot. You don't need to slow down to change lanes.

  • On a dry road, there is a big enough gap in the next lane. How much must you slow down to change lanes?

    • Not at all.
    • About 5 mph.
    • About 10 mph.
    Answer

    Not at all.

    If there is enough space in the next lane, you don't need to slow down to change lanes.

  • Traffic is heavy on a multilane road. What is the safest way to drive?

    • Switch to whichever lane is moving fastest.
    • Change lanes often to keep a steady speed.
    • Stay in one lane as much as you can.
    Answer

    Stay in one lane as much as you can.

  • Halfway through a turn, you realize it is the wrong street. What should you do?

    • Stop and back out of the turn.
    • Finish the turn and find another route.
    • Stop where you are and rethink.
    Answer

    Finish the turn and find another route.

    Once you start through an intersection or a turn, follow through. Correct your route later.

  • You pass the street where you meant to turn. What should you do?

    • Stop and back up to the corner you missed.
    • Keep going until you can turn around safely.
    • Turn right away, even from the wrong lane.
    Answer

    Keep going until you can turn around safely.

Types of Lanes

  • How can you tell how many people you need to use an HOV lane?

    • It is always at least three people, by law.
    • It is listed on your registration card.
    • Signs at the on-ramp or along the road.
    Answer

    Signs at the on-ramp or along the road.

  • May a motorcyclist riding alone use an HOV lane?

    • No, only cars carrying passengers may.
    • Only with a special DMV decal.
    • Yes, unless a sign says otherwise.
    Answer

    Yes, unless a sign says otherwise.

    Motorcycles may use HOV lanes unless otherwise posted.

  • How is the road surface of an HOV lane marked?

    • With a diamond and the words Carpool Lane.
    • With green paint and a bicycle symbol.
    • With a row of white triangles.
    Answer

    With a diamond and the words Carpool Lane.

    • A single solid yellow line on each side.
    • A broken inner line and a solid outer line.
    • Double solid yellow lines on both of its sides.
    Answer

    A broken inner line and a solid outer line.

  • What may you use a center left turn lane for?

    • To pass slow traffic when it is clear.
    • Only to start a left turn or a U-turn.
    • As a regular lane when traffic is heavy.
    Answer

    Only to start a left turn or a U-turn.

  • What is the farthest you may drive in a center left turn lane?

    • 100 feet.
    • 200 feet.
    • 500 feet.
    Answer

    200 feet.

  • You are driving slowly on a two-lane road where passing is unsafe. What is the fewest vehicles behind you that requires you to use a turnout?

    • Three.
    • Ten.
    • Five.
    Answer

    Five.

  • When may you drive in a bicycle lane?

    • To enter or leave the road.
    • To pass slower traffic on the right side.
    • Whenever no bicyclists are in sight ahead.
    Answer

    To enter or leave the road.

  • How does a bike lane's line usually change near an intersection?

    • The solid white line becomes dashed.
    • The solid white line becomes a double line.
    • The white line turns yellow.
    Answer

    The solid white line becomes dashed.

  • What do chevrons or diagonal stripes beside a bike lane do?

    • Mark a place to stop for loading.
    • Give more space from traffic and parked cars.
    • Mark an extra lane for turning cars.
    Answer

    Give more space from traffic and parked cars.

    A buffered bike lane uses chevrons or diagonal markings to put more space between bicyclists, traffic and parked cars.

  • What is a bike route?

    • A lane on which only bicycles may ride.
    • A preferred route on streets shared with cars.
    • A path separated from cars by a curb.
    Answer

    A preferred route on streets shared with cars.

  • You see a bicycle symbol with arrows painted in your traffic lane. What does it mean?

    • Only bicycles may use this lane.
    • Bicycles must stay on the shoulder here.
    • Bicyclists may ride in the lane with you.
    Answer

    Bicyclists may ride in the lane with you.

Turns

  • What should you do to make a right turn at an upcoming intersection?

    • Merge into the bicycle lane before making the turn.
    • Wait until the bicycle lane ends, then make the turn.
    • Make the turn from your current lane and do not enter the bicycle lane.
    Answer

    Merge into the bicycle lane before making the turn.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #3

  • How far before a turn should you start signaling?

    • About 100 feet.
    • About 50 feet.
    • About 25 feet.
    Answer

    About 100 feet.

  • As you turn right, where should you watch for bicyclists and pedestrians?

    • Between your vehicle and the curb.
    • Only in the crosswalk ahead of you.
    • In the lane to your left.
    Answer

    Between your vehicle and the curb.

  • You have signaled for a turn at an intersection. What should you do as you approach it?

    • Speed up so you clear the turn quickly.
    • Look over your shoulder and slow down.
    • Rely on your mirrors without turning your head.
    Answer

    Look over your shoulder and slow down.

  • What is a limit line?

    • A wide white line showing where to stop.
    • A yellow line marking where passing ends.
    • A row of white triangles where you yield.
    Answer

    A wide white line showing where to stop.

  • There is no limit line, but there is a crosswalk. Where should you stop?

    • On the crosswalk, short of the cross street.
    • Before the crosswalk.
    • Even with the STOP sign.
    Answer

    Before the crosswalk.

  • Before turning at an intersection, in what order should you look?

    • Left, right, then left again.
    • Right, left, then right again.
    • Left, then right.
    Answer

    Left, right, then left again.

  • You are stopped at this signal. When may you turn right?

    • After a full stop, if it is clear.
    • After the red arrow turns green.
    • After yielding to pedestrians.
    Answer

    After the red arrow turns green.

  • You want to drop off a passenger, and the only free curb is along a bus-only lane. What should you do?

    • Find somewhere else to stop.
    • Stop briefly with your hazards on.
    • Stop there if no bus is in sight.
    Answer

    Find somewhere else to stop.

  • When may you cross a bus-only lane?

    • To pass slow traffic.
    • To pick up a passenger.
    • To make a right turn.
    Answer

    To make a right turn.

    You may cross a public transit bus lane to make a right turn, but you may not otherwise drive, stop or park in it.

  • Your right turn lane doesn't merge into another lane and has no light or sign on its curb. Through traffic has a red light. What may you do?

    • Stop fully first, as for any right turn on red.
    • Wait until the through traffic light turns green.
    • Turn without stopping.
    Answer

    Turn without stopping.

    A dedicated right turn lane lets you turn without stopping even when through traffic has a red light, but you must obey any light or sign on its right curb.

Left Turns

  • A left turn lane begins ahead, bordered by a solid line. When should you enter it?

    • At its opening, without crossing the line.
    • As soon as there is room, crossing the line.
    • Only after your light has turned green.
    Answer

    At its opening, without crossing the line.

  • You are waiting to turn left while oncoming traffic passes. Which way should your front wheels point?

    • Straight ahead until you can turn.
    • To the left, ready to turn.
    • To the right, toward the curb.
    Answer

    Straight ahead until you can turn.

  • When may you turn left on a red light, after stopping?

    • From one one-way street onto another.
    • From a two-way street onto a one-way street.
    • Never, under any condition.
    Answer

    From one one-way street onto another.

U-Turn

  • Which lane should you use to make a U-turn?

    • The far-right lane, to swing wide.
    • The left turn lane or the far-left lane.
    • Any lane, once you have signaled.
    Answer

    The left turn lane or the far-left lane.

  • When may you make a U-turn in a residential district?

    • Only at an intersection with a signal.
    • When no vehicles are coming within 200 feet.
    • When no vehicles are coming within 100 feet.
    Answer

    When no vehicles are coming within 200 feet.

  • May you make a U-turn at an intersection on a green light?

    • No, U-turns at traffic signals are illegal.
    • Yes, unless a NO U-TURN sign is posted.
    • Only if you are in a residential district.
    Answer

    Yes, unless a NO U-TURN sign is posted.

  • How may you make a U-turn on a divided highway?

    • Across the center strip, if it is flat.
    • Only through an opening in the center divider.
    • Across two sets of double yellow lines.
    Answer

    Only through an opening in the center divider.

  • Where is it always illegal to make a U-turn?

    • Across a double yellow line.
    • On a railroad crossing.
    • On a quiet residential street.
    Answer

    On a railroad crossing.

    Never make a U-turn at or on a railroad crossing. U-turns across a double yellow line, or in a residential district when no one is within 200 feet, can be legal.

  • What is the least distance you must be able to see clearly in each direction before making a U-turn?

    • 200 feet.
    • 100 feet.
    • 500 feet.
    Answer

    200 feet.

  • You need to turn around. Which of these is illegal?

    • Using a fire station driveway.
    • Using a divider opening on a divided highway.
    • Making a U-turn at a green arrow.
    Answer

    Using a fire station driveway.

  • Where may you make a U-turn in a business district?

    • Anywhere, if no one is within 200 feet.
    • Only in the middle of a block.
    • Only at an intersection or a divider opening.
    Answer

    Only at an intersection or a divider opening.

    In a business district, U-turns are allowed only at intersections or at openings in a divided highway.

    • When signs or arrows show you may.

Braking

  • What is the smooth way to brake to a stop?

    • Keep on the gas, then brake firmly at the end.
    • Ease off the gas, then press the brake lightly.
    • Press the brake hard, then let up and repeat.
    Answer

    Ease off the gas, then press the brake lightly.

  • You stop at a limit line. Where should the front of your vehicle be?

    • On top of the line.
    • Behind the line.
    • Just past the line.
    Answer

    Behind the line.

  • You stop behind another vehicle. How much space should you leave?

    • About one foot from its bumper.
    • Enough to see its rear wheels.
    • At least three car lengths.
    Answer

    Enough to see its rear wheels.

    Leave enough space that you can see the rear wheels of the vehicle in front.

Merging and Exiting

  • What speed should you be driving when entering onto a highway?

    • At or near the speed of traffic.
    • Faster than the speed of traffic.
    • Slower than the speed of traffic.
    Answer

    At or near the speed of traffic.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #1

  • When should you stop on a freeway on-ramp before merging?

    • Only if it is absolutely necessary.
    • Always, to check for a gap.
    • Whenever a car is in the right lane.
    Answer

    Only if it is absolutely necessary.

    Merge when it is safe and don't stop unless absolutely necessary. Stopping makes it harder to reach the speed of traffic.

  • On a freeway ramp, a solid line separates your lane from the next one. When may you cross it?

    • Once the line ends or turns broken.
    • Once the next lane is clear.
    • Once you have signaled for five seconds.
    Answer

    Once the line ends or turns broken.

    Don't cross solid lines when merging onto or exiting a highway. Wait until the line ends, however clear the lane or long your signal.

  • You need to cross three lanes to reach your exit. How should you do it?

    • All at once, while the lanes are clear.
    • One lane at a time, signaling each time.
    • Slow down in your lane until a gap opens.
    Answer

    One lane at a time, signaling each time.

  • Signaling five seconds before a freeway exit covers about how much distance?

    • About 100 feet.
    • About 400 feet.
    • About 1,000 feet.
    Answer

    About 400 feet.

    Five seconds of signaling before an exit is about 400 feet.

  • To merge into traffic on a city street, about how big a gap do you need?

    • About one car length.
    • About a full block.
    • About half a block.
    Answer

    About half a block.

  • To merge onto the highway, about how big a gap do you need?

    • About a full block, or 300 feet.
    • About half a block, or 150 feet.
    • About two car lengths, or 30 feet.
    Answer

    About a full block, or 300 feet.

  • You are waiting to turn left. An oncoming car has its right signal on. What should you do?

    • Turn now; it is about to turn right.
    • Wait until it actually starts to turn.
    • Flash your headlights, then turn.
    Answer

    Wait until it actually starts to turn.

    Don't assume an oncoming car with its right signal on will turn before reaching you. Wait until it starts its turn.

Passing

  • To pass safely, what is the least distance a hill or curve ahead should be?

    • 100 feet.
    • One-tenth of a mile.
    • One-third of a mile.
    Answer

    One-third of a mile.

  • On a two-lane road you are nearing a curve and can't see around it. May you pass?

    • No, not until you can see ahead.
    • No, unless the car ahead is very slow.
    • Yes, if you pass quickly.
    Answer

    No, not until you can see ahead.

    Don't pass near a hill or curve if you can't see whether traffic is coming, however slow the car ahead. It is especially dangerous on one- and two-lane roads.

  • What is the closest to a bridge or railroad crossing you may pass another vehicle?

    • 50 feet.
    • 100 feet.
    • 300 feet.
    Answer

    100 feet.

  • Where should you not pass another vehicle?

    • On a one-way street, on the right.
    • At crossroads and driveways.
    • On an open highway with two lanes your way.
    Answer

    At crossroads and driveways.

    Don't pass at crossroads and driveways. Passing on the right is allowed on a one-way street or an open highway with two or more lanes your way.

  • The driver ahead is signaling a left turn. How may you pass?

    • On the right, if it is safe.
    • On the left, quickly.
    • On the right shoulder, if it is clear.
    Answer

    On the right, if it is safe.

  • When is it legal to drive off the road to pass another vehicle?

    • If the vehicle ahead is turning left.
    • It is not legal under any conditions.
    • If there are two or more one-way lanes.
    Answer

    It is not legal under any conditions.

Parking

    • Three feet longer than your car.
    • Exactly as long as your car.
    • Ten feet longer than your car.
    Answer

    Three feet longer than your car.

    • Drive forward into the space without stopping.
    • Stop next to the vehicle behind the open space, and then drive forward into the space.
    • Stop next to the vehicle in front of the open space, and then back into the space.
    Answer

    Stop next to the vehicle in front of the open space, and then back into the space.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #1

  • After parallel parking, what is the farthest from the curb your car should be?

    • 18 inches.
    • 3 feet.
    • 6 inches.
    Answer

    18 inches.

    You should end up parallel to and within 18 inches of the curb.

  • In addition to setting your parking brake, what should you do when parking on a hill?

    • Make sure the front vehicle wheels are parallel to the road.
    • Make sure your vehicle is left in the “neutral” position.
    • Make sure your vehicle is in the “park” position or in gear.
    Answer

    Make sure your vehicle is in the “park” position or in gear.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #2

  • You park headed downhill next to a curb. Which way should you turn your front wheels?

    • Away from the curb.
    • Into the curb.
    • Straight ahead.
    Answer

    Into the curb.

    Headed downhill, turn your front wheels into the curb so the car rolls into it, not into traffic.

  • When you park on a hill, in which case do you turn your front wheels away from the curb or road edge?

    • Headed downhill, next to a curb.
    • Headed uphill, next to a curb.
    • Headed uphill, with no curb.
    Answer

    Headed uphill, next to a curb.

    Only uphill with a curb do you turn the wheels away from it, letting the car roll back until a wheel touches the curb. Downhill, turn them into the curb; with no curb, turn them right, toward the edge.

  • You park on a hill with no curb. Which way should you turn your front wheels?

    • To the left, toward the center.
    • Straight ahead.
    • To the right, toward the edge.
    Answer

    To the right, toward the edge.

  • What does a white curb mean?

    • Park for the limited time shown on a sign.
    • Load or unload freight for the posted time.
    • Stop only to pick up or drop off passengers.
    Answer

    Stop only to pick up or drop off passengers.

    White curbs are for picking up or dropping off passengers only. Green is limited-time parking; yellow is loading.

  • What may you do at a green curb?

    • Stop only to pick up passengers.
    • Park for the limited time posted.
    • Park with no time limit.
    Answer

    Park for the limited time posted.

  • You drive an ordinary passenger car. What may you do at a yellow curb?

    • Park there for the whole day if you like.
    • Nothing at all; you may never stop there.
    • Load or unload, usually staying with your car.
    Answer

    Load or unload, usually staying with your car.

    Yellow curbs are for loading and unloading passengers or freight for the posted time; noncommercial drivers usually must stay with the vehicle.

  • What color curb does not allow vehicles to stop or park?

    • Yellow.
    • Red.
    • White.
    Answer

    Red.

Driving Green

  • Which habit helps you use less fuel?

    • Speeding up and slowing down smoothly.
    • Keeping your tires a little underinflated.
    • Carrying extra weight for better traction.
    Answer

    Speeding up and slowing down smoothly.

    Smooth, steady driving, properly inflated tires and less weight in the vehicle all improve fuel efficiency.

Law Enforcement Stops

  • An officer signals you to pull over. How should you show that you have seen them?

    • Turn on your hazard lights and stop.
    • Turn on your right turn signal.
    • Wave out of your window.
    Answer

    Turn on your right turn signal.

    Turn on your right turn signal to show the officer you see them, then pull over safely.

  • You are in the carpool lane when an officer signals you to pull over. Where should you stop?

    • On the left shoulder, beside the carpool lane.
    • On the right shoulder.
    • In the carpool lane itself.
    Answer

    On the right shoulder.

  • After pulling over for an officer, what should you do?

    • Stay in the car unless told to get out.
    • Get out and walk back to the officer.
    • Get out and wait beside your car.
    Answer

    Stay in the car unless told to get out.

  • As the officer walks up to your car, where should your hands be?

    • In clear view, such as on the wheel.
    • Reaching for your papers in the glove box.
    • In your lap, below the window.
    Answer

    In clear view, such as on the wheel.

  • When must an officer tell you the reason for a traffic stop?

    • Before questioning, except in an imminent threat.
    • Only if you ask, once they have your license.
    • When the stop ends, as they write the ticket.
    Answer

    Before questioning, except in an imminent threat.

  • When stopped by police, what must the driver show?

    • Your driver's license, nothing more.
    • License, insurance, and registration.
    • Your license and your vehicle's title.
    Answer

    License, insurance, and registration.

  • You don't produce your license, insurance, and registration when stopped. What may officers do?

    • Nothing, until you find them yourself.
    • Do a limited search for them.
    • Impound your vehicle at once.
    Answer

    Do a limited search for them.

  • During a traffic stop, an officer tells your passenger to get out. Must they?

    • No, only the driver must.
    • Only if the officer gives a reason.
    • Yes, they must comply.
    Answer

    Yes, they must comply.

  • During a traffic stop in California, who may ask about your immigration status?

    • Only federal officers.
    • Any officer who stops you.
    • Local police, but not state officers.
    Answer

    Only federal officers.

  • You are recording a traffic stop without interfering. Which is true about your phone?

    • The officer can't take it for that alone.
    • The officer may delete what you recorded.
    • The officer may take it if you keep recording.
    Answer

    The officer can't take it for that alone.

  • You want to record a stop, but your phone is in the glove box. What should you do?

    • Reach for it quickly before the officer arrives.
    • Get out and record from beside the car.
    • Ask before reaching for it.
    Answer

    Ask before reaching for it.

  • You believe an officer is violating your rights. What should you do?

    • Object out loud, but don't resist.
    • Physically block the officer.
    • Drive away and report it later.
    Answer

    Object out loud, but don't resist.

    Even if you think your rights are being violated, don't physically resist or use violence. You may voice your objection.

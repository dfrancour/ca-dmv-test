Section 8

Safe Driving: practice questions

Be Aware of Your Surroundings

  • Which of the following is an example of a safe driving practice?

    • Staring only at the middle of the road.
    • Always keep your eyes moving to scan the surroundings.
    • Using your high-beam headlights in the fog
    Answer

    Always keep your eyes moving to scan the surroundings.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #1

  • What is the least distance ahead of your vehicle you should scan the road?

    • 3 seconds ahead.
    • 10 seconds ahead.
    • 30 seconds ahead.
    Answer

    10 seconds ahead.

    Keep your eyes moving and scan at least 10 seconds ahead. Three seconds is the following distance, not how far to look.

  • A car merges in too close in front of you. What should you do?

    • Brake hard right away to slow down.
    • Speed up and pass it on the left.
    • Take your foot off the gas to make space.
    Answer

    Take your foot off the gas to make space.

    Ease off the accelerator to open up space again.

  • Which of the following should you do if you are being followed by a tailgater?

    • Change lanes and allow the tailgater to pass.
    • Tap the brakes to signal you are moving at a slower pace.
    • Increase your speed to match the speed of the vehicle.
    Answer

    Change lanes and allow the tailgater to pass.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #4

  • When should you leave extra space in front of you behind a motorcycle?

    • When it rides on gravel or metal gratings.
    • When it is on smooth, dry pavement.
    • Only when it is carrying a passenger.
    Answer

    When it rides on gravel or metal gratings.

  • Where on the freeway should you avoid staying?

    • Three seconds behind the car ahead.
    • Alongside other cars, in their blind spots.
    • In the right lane at a steady speed.
    Answer

    Alongside other cars, in their blind spots.

  • A car is entering the freeway from a ramp next to your lane. What should you do?

    • Hold your speed, since it must yield.
    • Speed up to get past the ramp first.
    • Make space for it to merge.
    Answer

    Make space for it to merge.

  • Your light is green and cross traffic has a red light. Should you still look both ways?

    • No, cross traffic must stop.
    • Only if a pedestrian is near.
    • Yes, look both ways anyway.
    Answer

    Yes, look both ways anyway.

  • When you look over your shoulder to check a blind spot, what should you turn?

    • Only your head.
    • Your head and shoulders, with the wheel.
    • Your whole body, toward the back seat.
    Answer

    Only your head.

Understand the Road Conditions

  • When should you use your high beams?

    • Close behind another car, to see past it.
    • On open country roads at night, where legal.
    • In rain at night, to light up the road.
    Answer

    On open country roads at night, where legal.

    Use high beams on open country roads or dark streets where legal. Use low beams in rain, and dim them within 300 feet of a car you are following.

  • At night, how fast should you drive?

    • At the posted limit, if the road is dry and clear.
    • Exactly 10 mph under the posted limit.
    • Slowly enough to stop within your headlights' reach.
    Answer

    Slowly enough to stop within your headlights' reach.

    At night, make sure you can stop within the distance your headlights light up.

  • An oncoming car's headlights are blinding you. Where should you look?

    • Straight at the lights to judge distance.
    • Toward the right edge of your lane.
    • Toward the center line of the road.
    Answer

    Toward the right edge of your lane.

  • An oncoming driver won't dim their high beams. What should you do?

    • Switch your high beams on in return.
    • Keep your low beams on.
    • Turn your headlights off for a moment.
    Answer

    Keep your low beams on.

  • You pull out of a brightly lit gas station onto a dark road. What should you do?

    • Drive slowly until your eyes adjust.
    • Speed up to match traffic right away.
    • Use your high beams until you can see.
    Answer

    Drive slowly until your eyes adjust.

    After leaving a bright place at night, drive slowly until your eyes adjust to the dark.

  • At night, a vehicle with only one light is coming toward you. What should you do?

    • Drive as far right as you can.
    • Flash your high beams at it.
    • Move toward the center line to see it.
    Answer

    Drive as far right as you can.

Sun Glare

  • Which of these helps you manage sun glare?

    • Speeding up to get out of the glare sooner.
    • Polarized sunglasses and a clean windshield.
    • Cleaning only the outside of the windshield.
    Answer

    Polarized sunglasses and a clean windshield.

  • To reduce problems with sun glare, when should you try not to drive?

    • At midday.
    • At sunrise and sunset.
    • On overcast days.
    Answer

    At sunrise and sunset.

    Glare is worst when the sun is low, so try to avoid driving at sunrise and sunset.

Skids

  • On an icy road, when should you slow down for a sharp curve?

    • Before the curve and while going through it.
    • Once you are partway into the curve.
    • Before the curve, then speed up through it.
    Answer

    Before the curve and while going through it.

  • Before going down a steep, slippery hill, what should you do?

    • Shift into neutral and coast.
    • Shift into a low gear.
    • Rely on the brakes alone.
    Answer

    Shift into a low gear.

  • Your car starts to skid. What should you do?

    • Brake hard and steer the opposite way.
    • Ease off the gas and steer into the skid.
    • Keep on the gas and hold the wheel straight.
    Answer

    Ease off the gas and steer into the skid.

  • What usually causes a locked-wheel skid?

    • Turning the wheel too sharply.
    • Speeding up too quickly on a curve.
    • Braking too hard at too high a speed.
    Answer

    Braking too hard at too high a speed.

  • Your car has four-wheel ABS and goes into a locked-wheel skid. What should you do?

    • Pump the brakes quickly.
    • Press firmly on the brake pedal.
    • Take your foot off the brake.
    Answer

    Press firmly on the brake pedal.

    With four-wheel ABS, apply firm pressure to the brake pedal. Pumping is for cars without ABS.

  • You are driving a car without ABS, and it starts to skid as you brake. What should you do?

    • Hold firm, steady pressure on the brake.
    • Pump the brakes until you reach a safe speed.
    • Shift into neutral and coast to a stop.
    Answer

    Pump the brakes until you reach a safe speed.

    Without ABS, quickly pump the brakes until you reach a safe speed. Firm, steady pressure is the method for four-wheel ABS.

  • You press the brake and the pedal sinks to the floor. What should you do first?

    • Hold the pedal down firmly.
    • Shift into neutral and coast.
    • Pump the brakes quickly.
    Answer

    Pump the brakes quickly.

  • Pumping your failed brakes doesn't help. What should you do next?

    • Downshift, then try the emergency brake.
    • Turn off the engine to slow down.
    • Shift into park to stop at once.
    Answer

    Downshift, then try the emergency brake.

  • After driving through deep water, how can you dry your brakes?

    • Press the gas and brake lightly at the same time.
    • Brake hard several times at high speed.
    • Park for a few minutes with the engine off.
    Answer

    Press the gas and brake lightly at the same time.

Slippery Roads

  • On a wet road, how much should you reduce your speed?

    • By 5 to 10 mph.
    • By half.
    • To 5 mph.
    Answer

    By 5 to 10 mph.

    Slow down by 5 to 10 mph on a wet road. Packed snow calls for half your speed, and ice for no more than 5 mph.

  • On packed snow, how much should you reduce your speed?

    • By 5 to 10 mph.
    • To no more than 5 mph.
    • By half.
    Answer

    By half.

    On packed snow, cut your speed by half. On a wet road it's 5 to 10 mph slower; on ice, no more than 5 mph.

  • On ice, what is the fastest you should drive?

    • 15 mph.
    • 25 mph.
    • 5 mph.
    Answer

    5 mph.

  • On a cold, wet day, where are icy spots most likely?

    • On bridges and in shady areas.
    • On sunny, open stretches of road.
    • On freshly paved, dark asphalt.
    Answer

    On bridges and in shady areas.

  • At what point during a rainfall are roads slippery on a hot day?

    • Immediately after it has stopped raining.
    • For the first several minutes.
    • When it has been raining for a few hours.
    Answer

    For the first several minutes.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #2

  • In a heavy storm you can't see farther than 100 feet. What is the fastest safe speed?

    • 40 mph.
    • 30 mph.
    • 45 mph.
    Answer

    30 mph.

  • It isn't safe to drive faster than 30 mph when you can't see farther than:

    • 100 feet.
    • 300 feet.
    • 500 feet.
    Answer

    100 feet.

    If heavy rain or snow limits your view to 100 feet, keep below 30 mph.

Hydroplaning

  • What is hydroplaning?

    • Brakes that stop working once they get wet.
    • Tires riding on water, not touching the road.
    • Spray from other cars blocking your view.
    Answer

    Tires riding on water, not touching the road.

    Hydroplaning is when the tires lose all contact with the road and ride on water. A small turn, brake or gust can then cause a skid.

  • On a wet road, you hear sloshing from your tires. What should you do?

    • Keep your speed and hold the wheel steady.
    • Slow down.
    • Change lanes quickly.
    Answer

    Slow down.

    Sloshing is a sign of hydroplaning risk. Slow down, and slow down when changing lanes or direction too.

  • Your car starts to hydroplane. What should you do?

    • Brake firmly to get back in control fast.
    • Turn the wheel sharply toward dry road.
    • Slow down gradually, without braking.
    Answer

    Slow down gradually, without braking.

    Slow down gradually and don't use the brakes; sudden braking can make you lose control.

Stuck in Snow or Mud

  • Your car is stuck in mud. How should you try to drive out?

    • Plenty of gas, so the wheels spin free.
    • Low gear, wheels straight, gentle gas.
    • High gear, with the wheels turned sharply.
    Answer

    Low gear, wheels straight, gentle gas.

  • When may you put boards or branches under your tires to get unstuck?

    • While the wheels spin slowly.
    • While you rock the car forward.
    • Only while the car is stopped.
    Answer

    Only while the car is stopped.

Flooded Roads

  • You reach a flooded road and can't tell how deep the water is. What should you do?

    • Drive through fast so you don't stall.
    • Find another route.
    • Follow the car ahead through it.
    Answer

    Find another route.

    You may not be able to judge a flood's depth by looking. If it might be deep, go another way.

  • You had to drive slowly through a flooded road. What should you do once you are through?

    • Test your brakes.
    • Pump the brakes hard at speed to dry them.
    • Drive on; the brakes dry by themselves.
    Answer

    Test your brakes.

High Winds

  • Which vehicles are most at risk in high winds?

    • Trucks, campers, and cars with trailers.
    • Small, light compact cars.
    • Low sports cars that catch less air.
    Answer

    Trucks, campers, and cars with trailers.

  • Strong gusts are pushing your car around. What should you do?

    • Keep your speed so gusts can't push you.
    • Slow down and grip the wheel firmly.
    • Hold the wheel loosely and ride with them.
    Answer

    Slow down and grip the wheel firmly.

    In high winds, slow down for better control and keep a firm hold on the wheel.

  • Should you use cruise control in high winds?

    • Yes; it keeps your speed steady.
    • Only on long, straight roads.
    • No; you need full use of the gas pedal.
    Answer

    No; you need full use of the gas pedal.

    Don't use cruise control in high winds, so you keep full control of the accelerator.

Fog or Heavy Smoke

  • Which lights should you use in heavy fog?

    • High-beam headlights.
    • Parking lights only.
    • Low-beam headlights.
    Answer

    Low-beam headlights.

    Use low beams in fog. High beams reflect back as glare, and driving with only parking or fog lights is illegal.

  • In fog, what should you do with your following distance?

    • Close it up to keep the taillights ahead in sight.
    • Keep it the same as usual.
    • Increase it.
    Answer

    Increase it.

  • Fog becomes too thick to drive safely. What should you do?

    • Stop in your lane and turn on your flashers.
    • Follow the taillights ahead of you closely.
    • Pull off the road and use your flashers.
    Answer

    Pull off the road and use your flashers.

Law Enforcement Traffic Breaks

  • A patrol car ahead turns on its emergency lights and slowly weaves across lanes. What should you do?

    • Brake hard and stop at once.
    • Pass the patrol car carefully.
    • Slow gradually to the officer's speed.
    Answer

    Slow gradually to the officer's speed.

    This is a traffic break. Slow down smoothly to the officer's speed, avoid sudden braking, and keep a safe distance.

  • During a law enforcement traffic break, what should you turn on?

    • Your emergency flashers.
    • Your high beams.
    • Your left turn signal.
    Answer

    Your emergency flashers.

  • When may you speed up after a traffic break?

    • When the officer's lights go off.
    • As soon as you see the hazard is gone.
    • Once you have passed the patrol car.
    Answer

    When the officer's lights go off.

Protect Yourself and Your Passengers

  • Who in your car must wear a seat belt?

    • You and anyone in the front seat.
    • You and every passenger.
    • Your passengers, but not you.
    Answer

    You and every passenger.

  • Your 15-year-old passenger isn't wearing a seat belt. Who can get the ticket?

    • The passenger.
    • You, the driver.
    • The passenger's parent.
    Answer

    You, the driver.

    The driver can be ticketed if a passenger under 16 isn't wearing a seat belt.

  • In a crash, your car suddenly stops. Without a seat belt, what happens to you?

    • You stop at the same moment as the car.
    • You keep moving at the same speed.
    • You are pushed back into your seat.
    Answer

    You keep moving at the same speed.

  • How should you wear the shoulder harness?

    • Under your arm, so it is more comfortable.
    • Behind your back, with the lap belt fastened.
    • Across your chest, with little or no slack.
    Answer

    Across your chest, with little or no slack.

  • Where should your lap belt sit?

    • Across your stomach, above the hips.
    • Loose, so you can move freely.
    • Snug and low across your hips.
    Answer

    Snug and low across your hips.

  • If you are pregnant, where should you wear the lap belt?

    • Across the middle of your abdomen.
    • Not at all; use the shoulder strap only.
    • As low as possible, under your abdomen.
    Answer

    As low as possible, under your abdomen.

  • A child is under 2 years old, under 40 pounds, and shorter than 3 feet 4 inches. How must the child ride?

    • In a rear-facing child seat.
    • In a forward-facing child seat.
    • In a booster seat with a lap belt.
    Answer

    In a rear-facing child seat.

  • Your car has front airbags. Where may a child in a rear-facing seat ride?

    • In a back seat, not the front.
    • In the front, once the child is 1.
    • In the front, if strapped in tightly.
    Answer

    In a back seat, not the front.

  • A 6-year-old who is 4 feet tall is riding with you. Where and how must the child ride?

    • In a child restraint in a back seat.
    • In a seat belt in any seat.
    • In a seat belt in the front seat.
    Answer

    In a child restraint in a back seat.

  • What is the shortest a 7-year-old may be to use a safety belt instead of a child seat?

    • 4 feet 9 inches.
    • 4 feet.
    • 5 feet 2 inches.
    Answer

    4 feet 9 inches.

  • When may a child under 8 ride in the front seat in a child restraint?

    • When the trip is only a short one.
    • When the vehicle has no rear seat.
    • When an adult is sitting in the back.
    Answer

    When the vehicle has no rear seat.

  • Whatever their height, at what age may children start using a safety belt instead of a child seat?

    • 6 years old.
    • 10 years old.
    • 8 years old.
    Answer

    8 years old.

  • What is the closest you should sit to the steering wheel air bag?

    • 4 inches.
    • 10 inches.
    • 18 inches.
    Answer

    10 inches.

  • You need to pop into a store for two minutes, and your 5-year-old would be left alone in the car. What should you do?

    • Bring the child along with you.
    • Lock the doors and leave them inside.
    • Leave them if the car stays in sight.
    Answer

    Bring the child along with you.

    It is illegal to leave a child six or younger unattended in a vehicle, for any length of time, even with the doors locked.

  • Who is the youngest person you may leave in charge of a child six or younger in a vehicle?

    • 12 years old.
    • 10 years old.
    • 16 years old.
    Answer

    12 years old.

  • It is a hot, sunny day. May you leave your dog in the parked car with a window slightly open?

    • No, not even with a window open.
    • No, not for more than ten minutes.
    • Yes; a cracked window keeps it cool.
    Answer

    No, not even with a window open.

Manage Your Speed

  • Which of the following is a requirement of California’s Basic Speed Law?

    • Always drive the speed limit, regardless of conditions.
    • Match your speed to that of your surrounding traffic.
    • Never drive faster than is safe for current conditions.
    Answer

    Never drive faster than is safe for current conditions.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #1

  • When you are driving the speed limit, you can be given a speeding ticket:

    • If road or weather conditions require a slower speed.
    • Under no circumstances because it is always legal.
    • Only if you are approaching a sharp curve in the road.
    Answer

    If road or weather conditions require a slower speed.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #4

  • A highway has a posted speed limit of 65 mph. What does this mean?

    • You must always drive 65 mph on that highway.
    • You may drive faster only if there are no other vehicles.
    • You must drive 65 mph only if driving conditions are ideal.
    Answer

    You must drive 65 mph only if driving conditions are ideal.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #3

  • Unless posted otherwise, what is the speed limit on a two-lane undivided highway?

    • 65 mph.
    • 55 mph.
    • 45 mph.
    Answer

    55 mph.

    The limit is 55 mph on two-lane undivided highways and for vehicles towing trailers, unless otherwise posted.

  • Unless posted otherwise, what is the speed limit for a vehicle towing a trailer?

    • 55 mph.
    • 65 mph.
    • 50 mph.
    Answer

    55 mph.

    Vehicles towing trailers are limited to 55 mph unless otherwise posted, even where cars may go 65.

Choose Between Hazards

  • On a two-lane road, a car is coming toward you and a bicyclist is ahead on your right. What should you do?

    • Speed up to pass the bicyclist before the car arrives.
    • Slow down, let the car pass, then pass the bicyclist.
    • Pass between them, keeping to the middle of your lane.
    Answer

    Slow down, let the car pass, then pass the bicyclist.

Know How to Handle Emergencies

  • A front tire blows out at freeway speed. What should you do?

    • Brake hard right away to stop quickly.
    • Lift your foot off the gas all at once.
    • Hold the wheel firmly and ease off the gas.
    Answer

    Hold the wheel firmly and ease off the gas.

  • Your right wheels drift off the pavement. What should you do?

    • Ease off the gas, then steer back carefully.
    • Jerk the wheel to get back on quickly.
    • Brake hard and swerve back at once.
    Answer

    Ease off the gas, then steer back carefully.

  • On a very hot day, your temperature gauge starts rising. What may help?

    • Turning the air conditioner up.
    • Turning off the air conditioner.
    • Driving faster to cool the engine.
    Answer

    Turning off the air conditioner.

  • Your car stops working on the freeway and you must get out. Which side should you use?

    • The driver's side, to watch the lanes.
    • Whichever side is closest to you.
    • The right side, away from traffic.
    Answer

    The right side, away from traffic.

  • Your car stalls on railroad tracks and a train is coming. Which way should you run?

    • Along the tracks, away from the train.
    • Away from the tracks, toward the train.
    • Away from the tracks, away from the train.
    Answer

    Away from the tracks, toward the train.

    Get out right away and run diagonally away from the tracks in the direction the train is coming from, so you aren't hit by flying debris. Then call 911.

  • Your car stalls on railroad tracks and no train is in sight. What should you do after getting out?

    • Wait in the car for a tow truck.
    • Call the number posted at the crossing.
    • Wait beside the car for a train to pass.
    Answer

    Call the number posted at the crossing.

Be Familiar with New Technology

  • What should you keep in mind about self-driving cars and cars with driver assistance?

    • They always stop for other road users.
    • They may react differently than a person would.
    • They never need the driver to pay attention.
    Answer

    They may react differently than a person would.

    Self-driving vehicles and cars with advanced driver assistance may respond to road situations differently than a human driver.

Be Aware of Carbon Monoxide

  • Why is the carbon monoxide in exhaust so dangerous?

    • It forms thick smoke that blocks your view.
    • It builds up only in old diesel engines.
    • It is deadly and has no smell.
    Answer

    It is deadly and has no smell.

  • Before you start your car inside a garage, what should you do?

    • Open the garage door.
    • Switch on the garage's vent fan.
    • Roll down the car's windows.
    Answer

    Open the garage door.

    Never start your vehicle in a garage with the door closed. Carbon monoxide builds up fast; a fan or open car windows don't make a closed garage safe.

  • While driving, you start yawning, feel dizzy, and have a headache and ringing ears. What could be the cause?

    • Low air pressure in your tires.
    • Glare from the sun on the windshield.
    • Carbon monoxide from your exhaust.
    Answer

    Carbon monoxide from your exhaust.

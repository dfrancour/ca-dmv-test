Section 9

Alcohol and Drugs: practice questions

Alcohol and Drugs

  • Do California's DUI laws apply to medicine prescribed by your doctor?

    • No, only to alcohol and illegal drugs.
    • Yes, if it affects your ability to drive.
    • Only if you take more than the prescribed dose.
    Answer

    Yes, if it affects your ability to drive.

    DUI law makes no distinction between illegal drugs and medicines from a doctor or pharmacy. Any drug that impairs your driving counts.

  • How does your tolerance to alcohol change as you get older?

    • It decreases.
    • It increases.
    • It stays the same.
    Answer

    It decreases.

  • Is it illegal to drive after taking a large amount of cough syrup that contains alcohol?

    • Yes; alcohol in any form counts.
    • No; medicines are exempt from DUI law.
    • Only if it was not prescribed to you.
    Answer

    Yes; alcohol in any form counts.

  • You had one beer and took an allergy pill. Together they make you drowsy. May you drive?

    • No; their combined effect is what counts.
    • No; you may never drive after taking any medicine.
    • Yes; each alone is within safe limits.
    Answer

    No; their combined effect is what counts.

  • An officer who suspects DUI asks you to take a blood test, and you refuse. What happens?

    • A one-year suspension or revocation.
    • Nothing, because the test is voluntary.
    • You get a warning noted on your record.
    Answer

    A one-year suspension or revocation.

  • A 15-year-old is convicted of riding a bicycle under the influence. What can happen?

    • Nothing; DUI laws don't cover bicycles.
    • A fine only, with no effect on driving.
    • A one-year delay in getting a license.
    Answer

    A one-year delay in getting a license.

Use or Possession of Alcohol or Cannabis Products in a Vehicle

  • When is it legal for a person to drive with an alcoholic beverage container that has been opened?

    • If the container is under the front seat.
    • If the container is in the trunk of the vehicle.
    • If the container is in the glove compartment.
    Answer

    If the container is in the trunk of the vehicle.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #4

  • You are driving your own car. May your passenger drink a beer or eat a cannabis edible?

    • No; the rule covers everyone in the car.
    • No; no alcohol may be in the car at all.
    • Yes, if the driver is sober.
    Answer

    No; the rule covers everyone in the car.

  • In which of these may a passenger have an open container of alcohol?

    • In the back seat of a car.
    • In the cab of a pickup.
    • In a taxi.
    Answer

    In a taxi.

    The open-container rules don't apply to passengers in a bus, taxi, camper or motorhome.

Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) Limits

  • What does BAC (blood alcohol concentration) measure?

    • How many drinks you have had.
    • The alcohol in your bloodstream.
    • How long since your last drink.
    Answer

    The alcohol in your bloodstream.

  • You are 25. What is the lowest BAC at which it is illegal for you to drive?

    • 0.05%.
    • 0.10%.
    • 0.08%.
    Answer

    0.08%.

  • You are 19. What is the lowest BAC at which it is illegal for you to drive?

    • 0.05%.
    • 0.08%.
    • 0.01%.
    Answer

    0.01%.

  • A 35-year-old is on DUI probation. What is the lowest BAC at which it is illegal for them to drive?

    • 0.01%.
    • 0.04%.
    • 0.08%.
    Answer

    0.01%.

  • When you drive a passenger for hire, what is the lowest BAC at which driving is illegal?

    • 0.08%.
    • 0.01%.
    • 0.04%.
    Answer

    0.04%.

  • Your BAC is below the legal limit. Is it safe to drive?

    • Not necessarily; alcohol affects you below it.
    • Yes; the limit is set at the safe level for all.
    • Yes, as long as you feel completely fine.
    Answer

    Not necessarily; alcohol affects you below it.

  • Can you be convicted of DUI without a BAC measurement?

    • Yes, depending on how impaired you are.
    • No, a BAC reading is always required.
    • Only if you refused to be tested.
    Answer

    Yes, depending on how impaired you are.

  • Which of these affects how high your BAC rises from drinking?

    • How long you have been driving.
    • The time of day you drink.
    • Your body weight and gender.
    Answer

    Your body weight and gender.

  • What is the fewest drinks that can affect your ability to drive safely?

    • Three.
    • One.
    • Enough to reach 0.08%.
    Answer

    One.

  • You are about to ride a jet ski on a lake. What does the law say about drinking alcohol?

    • You may not drink while you operate it.
    • You may drink, since you're off the road.
    • You may drink if you stay under 0.08%.
    Answer

    You may not drink while you operate it.

DUI Arrests

  • By driving in California, what have you agreed to?

    • A search of your car at any traffic stop.
    • A breath test at any traffic stop at all.
    • A chemical test if suspected of DUI.
    Answer

    A chemical test if suspected of DUI.

  • You took a breath test after being stopped for DUI. May you still be required to take a blood or urine test?

    • No, one test is the limit.
    • Yes, to check for drugs.
    • Only if your breath test was over 0.08%.
    Answer

    Yes, to check for drugs.

  • Under the Administrative Per Se law, what happens when you are arrested for DUI?

    • Nothing, until a court convicts you.
    • You get a warning letter from the DMV.
    • The DMV suspends your driving privilege.
    Answer

    The DMV suspends your driving privilege.

    Administrative Per Se requires the DMV to suspend your driving privilege when you are arrested for DUI, separate from any court case.

  • After a DUI arrest, the officer takes your license. What may you be given?

    • A 30-day temporary license.
    • A 10-day temporary license.
    • A 6-month restricted license.
    Answer

    A 30-day temporary license.

  • After a DUI arrest, how long do you have to request a DMV administrative hearing?

    • 10 days from the arrest.
    • 30 days from the arrest.
    • 5 days from the arrest.
    Answer

    10 days from the arrest.

DUI Convictions

  • If you are convicted of DUI, what must you complete?

    • A traffic violator school course.
    • A DUI program.
    • A new behind-the-wheel test.
    Answer

    A DUI program.

  • After a DUI conviction, which form must you file with the DMV?

    • An SR-1 traffic collision report.
    • A DS 699 driver reexamination request.
    • An SR 22 insurance proof certificate.
    Answer

    An SR 22 insurance proof certificate.

  • After a DUI conviction, what may you be required to install on your vehicle?

    • A speed-limiting device.
    • An ignition interlock device.
    • A dashboard camera.
    Answer

    An ignition interlock device.

  • A DUI conviction can bring a jail sentence of up to:

    • Six months.
    • 30 days.
    • Two years.
    Answer

    Six months.

    A DUI conviction can mean up to six months in jail, along with fines and other penalties.

  • How long does a DUI conviction stay on your driver's record?

    • 36 months.
    • Five years.
    • 10 years.
    Answer

    10 years.

    DUI convictions stay on your record for 10 years, and another DUI in that time can bring added penalties.

Drivers Under 21

  • A later test shows a 19-year-old driver's BAC is 0.05%. What may happen?

    • Arrest for DUI and license suspension.
    • Nothing, since it is under 0.08%.
    • A warning, since it is a first stop.
    Answer

    Arrest for DUI and license suspension.

  • A driver under 21 is convicted of DUI with a BAC of 0.01% or more. What may happen?

    • A 30-day suspension and no DUI program.
    • A fine only, since it's under 0.08%.
    • One-year revocation and a DUI program.
    Answer

    One-year revocation and a DUI program.

  • You are 19 and not working. When may you carry alcohol in your car?

    • Any time the container is sealed and unopened.
    • Only when it is locked in the trunk.
    • Only with someone 21 or older, sealed.
    Answer

    Only with someone 21 or older, sealed.

  • A driver under 21 is caught with alcohol in the car. How long can the car be impounded?

    • Up to 10 days.
    • Up to 30 days.
    • Up to one year.
    Answer

    Up to 30 days.

  • A driver under 21 caught with alcohol in the car may have their license suspended for:

    • 30 days.
    • Six months.
    • One year.
    Answer

    One year.

  • An officer suspects a 19-year-old driver has been drinking. What can the officer require?

    • A breath test or other chemical test.
    • Nothing, without proof of a 0.08% BAC.
    • A field walking test, but no breath test.
    Answer

    A breath test or other chemical test.

    If an officer suspects a driver under 21 has been drinking, they can require a hand-held breath test, PAS, or other chemical test.

Section 7

Laws and Rules of the Road: practice questions

Traffic Control

  • As you approach a traffic signal or sign, whom should you be ready to yield to?

    • Anyone who may have the right-of-way.
    • No one, once your light has turned green.
    • Pedestrians in marked crosswalks, and no others.
    Answer

    Anyone who may have the right-of-way.

  • No sign forbids it. What must you do before turning right at a solid red light?

    • Stop at the limit line and yield to pedestrians.
    • Slow down and yield, without needing to stop.
    • Wait for a green arrow pointing to the right.
    Answer

    Stop at the limit line and yield to pedestrians.

    A right turn on red is allowed unless a sign forbids it, but only after a full stop at the limit line and yielding to pedestrians.

  • The traffic light ahead is flashing like this. What should you do?

    • Slow down and go ahead with caution.
    • Stop and wait for the light to turn green.
    • Stop, then go when it is safe.
    Answer

    Stop, then go when it is safe.

    A flashing red light works like a STOP sign: stop, then proceed when it is safe. Slowing without stopping is the rule for a flashing yellow light.

  • The light changes to this as you near the intersection. What should you do?

    • Speed up to clear the intersection before red.
    • Stop at once, even if it means braking hard.
    • Stop if you can do so safely.
    Answer

    Stop if you can do so safely.

  • You are turning left on a green arrow, already in the intersection, when the arrow changes to this. What should you do?

    • Stop where you are and wait for the next arrow.
    • Complete your turn with caution.
    • Speed up to beat the red light.
    Answer

    Complete your turn with caution.

  • What does a flashing yellow traffic signal light direct a driver to do?

    • Stop and proceed when the traffic allows you to proceed safely.
    • Slow down and proceed with caution.
    • Stop and wait for a flashing green signal.
    Answer

    Slow down and proceed with caution.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #4

  • You want to turn left, and this arrow is flashing. What should you do?

    • Turn; oncoming traffic is stopped for you.
    • Turn after yielding to oncoming traffic.
    • Stop and wait for a solid green arrow.
    Answer

    Turn after yielding to oncoming traffic.

    A flashing yellow arrow allows the turn but does not protect it, so yield to oncoming traffic first. Only a green arrow means oncoming traffic is stopped.

  • If a traffic signal is green, but traffic is blocking the intersection, what should you do?

    • Partially enter the intersection, as allowed by traffic.
    • Wait and do not enter the intersection until your vehicle can get completely across.
    • Drive around the traffic on the shoulder to help ease the congestion.
    Answer

    Wait and do not enter the intersection until your vehicle can get completely across.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #3

  • You are in a left-turn lane and the signal shows this. What does it tell you?

    • You may turn; oncoming traffic is stopped.
    • You may turn after yielding to oncoming cars.

    Answer

    You may turn; oncoming traffic is stopped.

  • You reach an intersection where the traffic lights are not working. What should you do?

    • Slow down and yield, as at a YIELD sign.
    • Go through if the main road looks clear.
    • Stop, as you would at an all-way STOP.
    Answer

    Stop, as you would at an all-way STOP.

Pedestrian Signals and Signs

  • A pedestrian signal shows a steady raised hand. What does it mean?

    • Pedestrians may cross only within the crosswalk.
    • Pedestrians may not cross the street.
    • Pedestrians may cross if they hurry.
    Answer

    Pedestrians may not cross the street.

    A steady Don't Walk or raised hand means pedestrians may not cross.

  • A pedestrian signal shows a flashing raised hand. What does it tell pedestrians?

    • Start crossing now; the walk signal is next.
    • Start crossing only if no cars are turning.
    • Do not start crossing; the light is about to change.
    Answer

    Do not start crossing; the light is about to change.

    A flashing Don't Walk or raised hand warns that the light is about to change, so pedestrians should not start to cross.

  • What should you do if a pedestrian is still in the crosswalk after the “DON”T WALK” sign begins to flash and your light has turned green?

    • Wait until the pedestrian is out of your path before proceeding.
    • Wait until the pedestrian signals that it is okay for you to proceed.
    • Wait until the pedestrian has crossed the street completely before proceeding.
    Answer

    Wait until the pedestrian has crossed the street completely before proceeding.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #4

  • What do the numbers on a pedestrian signal count down?

    • The seconds until the walk signal comes on.
    • The seconds until cars may turn right on red.
    • The seconds left to cross the street.
    Answer

    The seconds left to cross the street.

    Countdown numbers show pedestrians how many seconds they have left to cross.

  • When may pedestrians cross on a diagonal (crisscross) crosswalk?

    • Whenever no cars are in the intersection.
    • Only when the walk signal allows it.
    • Only when the vehicle lights are green.
    Answer

    Only when the walk signal allows it.

  • What are beeping or chirping sounds at a crossing for?

    • To warn drivers the light will change.
    • To help blind pedestrians cross.
    • To tell pedestrians their time is ending.
    Answer

    To help blind pedestrians cross.

  • What does pressing a pedestrian push button at a crossing do?

    • It turns every traffic light red at once.
    • It brings up the WALK signal.
    • It lets you cross against a red light.
    Answer

    It brings up the WALK signal.

    The push button activates the WALK or walking-person signal, which tells pedestrians they may cross.

  • At an intersection with no pedestrian signals, what do pedestrians obey?

    • The traffic signals for vehicles.
    • Their own sense of gaps in traffic.
    • Only STOP signs, where there are any.
    Answer

    The traffic signals for vehicles.

Signs

  • A sign warns of a hazard ahead, but it isn't diamond-shaped. How should you treat it?

    • Obey it; any warning sign must be obeyed.
    • Treat it as advice; warning signs aren't laws.
    • Obey it only if it is yellow.
    Answer

    Obey it; any warning sign must be obeyed.

    You must obey every warning sign, whatever its shape or color, not just diamond-shaped ones.

  • You reach this sign and the roads look empty. What must you do?

    • Slow down and roll through if it is safe.
    • Slow to a crawl and look both ways.
    • Stop fully and check every direction.
    Answer

    Stop fully and check every direction.

    A STOP sign always requires a full stop and a check of traffic in all directions, even when the road looks empty.

  • What should you do at this sign?

    • Slow down and be ready to stop.
    • Come to a full stop every time.
    • Slow down and yield only to cars.
    Answer

    Slow down and be ready to stop.

    A YIELD sign means slow down and be ready to stop to let vehicles, bicyclists or pedestrians pass first.

  • Just after turning onto a road, you see this sign facing you. What should you do?

    • Keep going carefully to the next exit.
    • Stop in your lane and wait for help.
    • Back out or turn around when it is safe.
    Answer

    Back out or turn around when it is safe.

  • Driving at night, you see the road reflectors shining red in your headlights. What does this mean?

    • You are going the wrong way.
    • A railroad crossing is just ahead.
    • A work zone is just ahead.
    Answer

    You are going the wrong way.

  • What does a sign like this one mean?

    • Do what the picture shows, with caution.
    • What the picture shows is just ahead.
    • You may not do what the picture shows.
    Answer

    You may not do what the picture shows.

    A red circle with a slash means the action in the picture is not allowed.

  • You see this sign ahead. What should you do?

    • Look, listen, slow down, and be ready to stop.
    • Stop at the sign, then go if no gate is down.
    • Keep your speed unless lights are flashing.
    Answer

    Look, listen, slow down, and be ready to stop.

  • At many railroad crossings, what does a blue and white sign tell you?

    • How far away the next train is.
    • What to do in an emergency at the tracks.
    • The speed limit for crossing the tracks.
    Answer

    What to do in an emergency at the tracks.

  • You see a five-sided sign ahead. What does it tell you?

    • A railroad crossing is ahead.
    • You are near a school.
    • A no-passing zone begins.
    Answer

    You are near a school.

  • What does a diamond-shaped sign warn you about?

    • Rules you must obey, such as speed limits.
    • Services such as gas and food ahead.
    • Specific road conditions and dangers ahead.
    Answer

    Specific road conditions and dangers ahead.

    Diamond-shaped signs warn of road conditions and dangers ahead. Rules you must obey are on white rectangular signs.

  • What does a white rectangular sign tell you?

    • A danger on the road ahead.
    • A rule you must obey.
    • Services such as gas and food.
    Answer

    A rule you must obey.

    White rectangular signs state rules you must obey. Diamond signs warn of dangers, and five-sided signs mark schools.

Right-of-Way Rules: Who Goes First?

  • Two vehicles approach an intersection. Which one generally has the right-of-way?

    • The one on the wider road.
    • The one that arrives first.
    • The one going straight.
    Answer

    The one that arrives first.

  • A driver who should yield to you doesn't seem to be slowing down. What should you do?

    • Give up your right-of-way to avoid a crash.
    • Keep going, since the right-of-way is yours.
    • Sound your horn and hold your speed.
    Answer

    Give up your right-of-way to avoid a crash.

    Never assume others will yield. Giving up your right-of-way to prevent a collision is the safe choice.

  • What makes an intersection uncontrolled?

    • It has STOP signs on only two corners.
    • It has no signs or traffic lights.
    • It has no marked crosswalks.
    Answer

    It has no signs or traffic lights.

  • At an intersection with no STOP or YIELD signs, a bicyclist on your right arrives at the same time as you. Who goes first?

    • The bicyclist.
    • You, because you are in a motor vehicle.
    • Whoever signals first.
    Answer

    The bicyclist.

  • You are on the road that ends at a T intersection with no STOP or YIELD sign. Who has the right-of-way?

    • You, if you arrived first.
    • Whoever is on the right.
    • Traffic on the through road.
    Answer

    Traffic on the through road.

  • What should a driver do who is stopped at an intersection and wants to make a left turn?

    • Turn immediately if there are no pedestrians.
    • Take the right-of-way turn before oncoming traffic.
    • Give the right-of-way to any approaching vehicle that is close enough to be dangerous.
    Answer

    Give the right-of-way to any approaching vehicle that is close enough to be dangerous.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #2

  • You are pulling out of a parking lot onto a busy street. Who has the right-of-way?

    • You, once your turn signal is on.
    • You, if you are turning right.
    • Traffic already on the street.
    Answer

    Traffic already on the street.

    When entering traffic, yield to vehicles already in the lanes and go only when it is safe.

  • In which direction do you travel in a roundabout?

    • Clockwise, around the center island.
    • Either way, by the shortest route.
    • Counter-clockwise, around the center island.
    Answer

    Counter-clockwise, around the center island.

  • As you approach a roundabout, whom must you yield to?

    • All traffic already in the circle.
    • Only traffic entering from your right.
    • No one; traffic in the circle yields to you.
    Answer

    All traffic already in the circle.

  • How do you enter a roundabout?

    • Turn right into it when there is a safe gap.
    • Turn left into it to take the shortest path.
    • Drive in and stop until the circle is clear.
    Answer

    Turn right into it when there is a safe gap.

  • Which of these is illegal inside a roundabout?

    • Changing lanes with a signal.
    • Going around more than once.
    • Passing another vehicle.
    Answer

    Passing another vehicle.

    You may not stop or pass in a roundabout. Signaling a lane change is expected, and going around again is how you return to a missed exit.

  • When should you use your turn signal in a roundabout?

    • Only as you enter it.
    • Never; signals confuse others there.
    • When changing lanes or exiting.
    Answer

    When changing lanes or exiting.

  • You miss your exit in a roundabout. What should you do?

    • Stop and back up to the exit.
    • Stop and wait for a gap to cut across.
    • Keep going around until you reach it again.
    Answer

    Keep going around until you reach it again.

    If you miss your exit, continue around the roundabout until you come back to it. Never stop or back up.

  • In a two-lane roundabout, which lanes should you use to turn right?

    • Enter in the left lane and exit right.
    • Either lane, then exit in the right lane.
    • Enter and exit in the right lane.
    Answer

    Enter and exit in the right lane.

    To turn right in a multilane roundabout, choose the right lane to enter and exit in the right lane.

  • To go straight through a two-lane roundabout, which lane may you use?

    • Only the left lane, moving right to exit.
    • Either lane, moving to the right lane to exit.
    • Either lane, exiting in the lane you entered.
    Answer

    Either lane, exiting in the lane you entered.

  • What should you do when reaching an intersection where a person operating a motorized wheelchair has entered the crosswalk?

    • Remain stopped behind the crosswalk line until the motorized wheelchair has safely finished crossing.
    • Remain stopped behind the nearest crosswalk line until the motorized wheelchair is beyond the range of your vehicle.
    • Assume right-of-way if the motorized wheelchair stops in the crosswalk.
    Answer

    Remain stopped behind the crosswalk line until the motorized wheelchair has safely finished crossing.

  • Which of these people counts as a pedestrian?

    • A person riding a bicycle.
    • A person driving a golf cart.
    • A person on a skateboard.
    Answer

    A person on a skateboard.

    Pedestrians include people walking and people on things other than vehicles or bicycles, such as skateboards or roller skates.

  • Pedestrians have the right-of-way at intersections. Do they also have to follow the rules of the road?

    • No, only drivers must follow them.
    • Only when they are outside a crosswalk.
    • Yes, they must follow them too.
    Answer

    Yes, they must follow them too.

  • A car in the lane next to you is stopped at a crosswalk. What should you do?

    • Pass slowly while ready to stop.
    • Do not pass it; someone may be crossing.
    • Pass it if your light is green.
    Answer

    Do not pass it; someone may be crossing.

    Never pass a vehicle stopped at a crosswalk. It may hide a pedestrian who is crossing.

  • A pedestrian waiting at the curb makes eye contact with you. What does it usually mean?

    • They are waving you through first.
    • They are ready to cross; yield to them.
    • They have seen you and will wait.
    Answer

    They are ready to cross; yield to them.

    Eye contact usually means a pedestrian is ready to cross, so yield to them.

  • A pedestrian starts across at a corner where the crosswalk has no painted lines. Who has the right-of-way?

    • You, because the crosswalk is unmarked.
    • Whoever reaches the spot first.
    • The pedestrian.
    Answer

    The pedestrian.

  • You approach a crosswalk with flashing lights, but the lights are not flashing. What should you do?

    • Keep going, since no one is crossing.
    • Stop and wait for the lights to flash.
    • Look for pedestrians and be ready to stop.
    Answer

    Look for pedestrians and be ready to stop.

  • A pedestrian with a white cane starts across the street against your green light. Who has the right-of-way?

    • You, because your light is green.
    • The pedestrian, but only in a crosswalk.
    • The pedestrian, at all times.
    Answer

    The pedestrian, at all times.

  • Why take extra care when backing up near a blind pedestrian in a hybrid or electric car?

    • They rely on sound, and it is quiet.
    • Its headlights are harder for them to see.
    • It speeds up faster than a gas car.
    Answer

    They rely on sound, and it is quiet.

    Blind pedestrians rely on sound to know a vehicle is near, and hybrid and electric vehicles make little noise.

  • A blind pedestrian is slow to finish crossing in front of you. What should you do?

    • Tap your horn so they know you are there.
    • Drive slowly around them.
    • Wait quietly without using your horn.
    Answer

    Wait quietly without using your horn.

    Never honk at a blind person; it can confuse them. Wait until they have crossed.

  • At an intersection, a blind pedestrian pulls in their cane and steps back from the curb. What does this usually mean?

    • They are about to cross.
    • They need help crossing.
    • You may go.
    Answer

    You may go.

  • On a steep, narrow road you meet another car and neither can pass. You are facing downhill. What should you do?

    • Wait for the other driver to back down.
    • Hold your place; downhill traffic goes first.
    • Back up until the other car can pass.
    Answer

    Back up until the other car can pass.

    The vehicle facing uphill has the right-of-way. The downhill vehicle backs up, since it has more control backing up the hill.

    • About 300 feet.

    About 300 feet.

  • At 55 mph, how far can it take a large truck to stop?

    • Up to 400 feet.
    • Up to 300 feet.
    • Up to 200 feet.
    Answer

    Up to 400 feet.

    Large vehicles take longer to stop than cars at the same speed: up to 400 feet at 55 mph, against about 300 feet for a car.

Turning

  • A truck ahead with its right signal on swings left before reaching the corner. Why?

    • It needs room to make a wide right turn.
    • It has changed its mind and is turning left.
    • It is moving over to let you pass.
    Answer

    It needs room to make a wide right turn.

    A truck's rear wheels follow a shorter path than its front wheels, so it may swing wide the other way to turn. Check its signals before passing.

Motorcycles

  • Which is true about motorcyclists on the road?

    • They have the same rights and duties as drivers.
    • They must always yield to cars and trucks.
    • They must ride on the right shoulder.
    Answer

    They have the same rights and duties as drivers.

  • How much following distance should you leave behind a motorcycle?

    • At least one car length.
    • At least two seconds.
    • At least three seconds.
    Answer

    At least three seconds.

    Leave at least three seconds so you can avoid the rider if they brake suddenly or fall.

  • A motorcycle rides between lanes of slow traffic. Is this legal in California?

    • No; it is never legal.
    • Only on roads posted to allow it.
    • Yes; this is called lane splitting.
    Answer

    Yes; this is called lane splitting.

    It is legal in California for motorcycles to share lanes with other vehicles. This is called lane splitting.

  • You want to pass a motorcycle. How should you do it?

    • Use the next lane; never share its lane.
    • Squeeze past within its lane if it is wide.
    • Pass on the shoulder to give it room.
    Answer

    Use the next lane; never share its lane.

  • You have parked next to traffic. What should you do before opening your door?

    • Open it slowly so riders will notice it.
    • Check for motorcyclists and other traffic.
    • Open it quickly and get out fast.
    Answer

    Check for motorcyclists and other traffic.

    Check for motorcyclists before opening a door next to traffic; a rider may have no room to avoid it.

  • A motorcycle behind you wants to pass. How can you help?

    • Move to one side of your lane.
    • Speed up so it can merge in behind you.
    • Brake sharply to let it by sooner.
    Answer

    Move to one side of your lane.

    NEV signs and markings mean neighborhood electric vehicles use the road, so watch for slow-moving vehicles.

  • What is the highest speed limit on a road that a low-speed vehicle may use?

    • 35 mph.
    • 25 mph.
    • 45 mph.
    Answer

    35 mph.

    NEVs and LSVs top out at 25 mph and are kept off roads with speed limits over 35 mph.

  • What is the top speed of a neighborhood electric vehicle (NEV)?

    • 15 mph.
    • 35 mph.
    • 25 mph.
    Answer

    25 mph.

    NEVs and LSVs reach at most 25 mph. They may not use roads posted above 35 mph.

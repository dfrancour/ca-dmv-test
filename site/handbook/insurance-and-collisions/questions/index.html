Section 10

Financial Responsibility, Insurance Requirements, and Collisions: practice questions

Financial Responsibility, Insurance Requirements, and Collisions

  • When must you have proof of insurance with you?

    • Whenever you drive.
    • Only after you have been in a collision.
    • When you renew registration, not day to day.
    Answer

    Whenever you drive.

  • After a collision, what must you show the other driver?

    • Only your name and phone number, nothing else.
    • Nothing, unless the police come to the scene.
    • License, registration, insurance, and address.
    Answer

    License, registration, insurance, and address.

Insurance Requirements

  • What is the least your insurance may cover for the death or injury of one person?

    • $15,000.
    • $60,000.
    • $30,000.
    Answer

    $30,000.

    Minimum coverage is $30,000 for one person's death or injury, $60,000 for more than one person, and $15,000 for property damage.

  • What is the least your insurance may cover for the death or injury of more than one person?

    • $60,000.
    • $30,000.
    • $100,000.
    Answer

    $60,000.

  • What is the least your insurance may cover for property damage?

    • $15,000.
    • $5,000.
    • $30,000.
    Answer

    $15,000.

    Minimum coverage for property damage is $15,000.

  • Before buying car insurance, what should you check?

    • The agent has been approved by the DMV.
    • The agent has an office in your own city.
    • The agent is licensed by the Department of Insurance.
    Answer

    The agent is licensed by the Department of Insurance.

Driving Without Insurance

  • You are in a collision without insurance, and it was not your fault. What can happen?

    • A suspension of up to four years.
    • Nothing, since you weren't at fault.
    • A fine, with no license suspension.
    Answer

    A suspension of up to four years.

Collisions

  • What is one of the most common causes of traffic collisions?

    • Paying attention to your surroundings.
    • Better traffic flow.
    • Driver distractions.
    Answer

    Driver distractions.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #2

  • You see a car's emergency flashers on the road ahead. What should you do?

    • Keep your speed and change lanes fast.
    • Stop behind it until it moves.
    • Slow down and pass with care.
    Answer

    Slow down and pass with care.

  • You bump another car in slow traffic and see no damage. What must you do?

    • Keep going, since nothing was damaged.
    • Stop; driving off would be a hit-and-run.
    • Wave, and drive on if the driver waves back.
    Answer

    Stop; driving off would be a hit-and-run.

    If you are in a collision you must stop. Leaving the scene is a hit-and-run, which is punished severely.

  • After a minor collision, no one is hurt and both cars still run. What should you do?

    • Move out of traffic, then call 911.
    • Leave the cars where they are for police.
    • Drive home and call the other driver later.
    Answer

    Move out of traffic, then call 911.

  • Someone was injured in your collision. How soon must it be reported to law enforcement?

    • Within 24 hours.
    • Within 10 days.
    • Within 72 hours.
    Answer

    Within 24 hours.

    Report injury or fatal collisions to law enforcement within 24 hours. The 10-day deadline is for the DMV report.

  • You hit a parked car and can't find the owner. What must you do?

    • Leave a note and report it to police.
    • Leave, since you tried to find them.
    • Leave a note, with no need to report it.
    Answer

    Leave a note and report it to police.

  • You hit a deer and it is injured. What should you do?

    • Call the humane society or police.
    • Move it off the road yourself.
    • Drive on; animals aren't reportable.
    Answer

    Call the humane society or police.

  • You are required to notify DMV by filing a Report of Traffic Accident Occuring in California (SR-1) form if:

    • You allowed a licensed driver from another state to drive your vehicle.
    • You were involved in a collision with more than $1,000 in damages.
    • You failed to pay your registration fees.
    Answer

    You were involved in a collision with more than $1,000 in damages.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #2

  • You are required to notify DMV by filing a Report of Traffic Accident Occuring in California (SR-1) form if:

    • You change your insurance company.
    • You were involved in a collision with an injury.
    • Your vehicle fails a smog test.
    Answer

    You were involved in a collision with an injury.

    From the DMV's Class C Test #3

  • Someone had a minor injury in your collision. What is the deadline for reporting it to the DMV?

    • 10 days.
    • 24 hours.
    • 30 days.
    Answer

    10 days.

    Report to the DMV within 10 days if anyone was hurt, even slightly, or if damage was over $1,000. Police must be told within 24 hours of an injury.

  • Your collision caused $2,000 in damage, and the other driver was at fault. Who must file an SR 1 with the DMV?

    • Each driver, at fault or not.
    • Only the driver at fault.
    • The officer at the scene.
    Answer

    Each driver, at fault or not.

    Each driver must file an SR 1, whether or not they caused the collision. Law enforcement will not make the report for you.

  • What happens if you don't file a required SR 1 collision report?

    • The police will file it for you.
    • Your license will be suspended.
    • Your insurer files it for a fee.
    Answer

    Your license will be suspended.
